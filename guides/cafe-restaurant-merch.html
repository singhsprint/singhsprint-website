Merch your customers will actually buy: a guide for cafés, restaurants and local brands

Staff uniforms and merch you sell are different jobs. Here is what to start with, how many to order, what a launch run really costs, and how to have it on the shelf by your date.

7 min read Singhs Print · Updated October 2026 Filed under: Retail merch, Businesses

More and more of the orders we print in the West Island are not uniforms. They are cafés, restaurants, bakeries, breweries and small local brands putting their name on things customers pay for: a tee by the register, a crewneck for the cold months, a cap, a tote. Done well, merch pays for itself and puts your logo on people walking around Montreal. Done badly, it is a box of the wrong sizes in the back room.

This guide covers the decisions that separate the two: which pieces to start with, which blanks feel like something worth buying, how many to order, and what it costs.

Merch is not a uniform

A staff uniform is judged on durability and cost per wear. Merch is judged in two seconds, by a customer holding it at the counter. That changes the choices:

  • The blank matters more than the print. A heavyweight, soft, garment-dyed tee feels like a product. A thin promotional tee feels like a giveaway, whatever is printed on it.
  • Colour is part of the design. Washed, muted colours (ivory, sage, pepper, terracotta) read as retail. Bright primary colours read as event shirts.
  • The design can be quieter. People buy merch from places they like. A small logo on the chest and a bigger graphic on the back usually outsells a giant logo on the front.

Get the design right for the method

Each piece above uses the method that suits it, and that shapes the artwork:

  • DTG on the tees and crewnecks. Direct-to-garment prints straight into cotton, so the print is soft and can hold full colour, gradients and fine detail. It is the right call for illustrated or multi-colour back graphics.
  • DTF on the totes. A DTF transfer sits on the surface and stays bright on canvas and on dark fabric.
  • Embroidery on the caps. Keep the cap mark simple: a symbol or a short word, not your full logo with a tagline. Small text is the first thing to fail in thread.

A common, effective layout is a small logo on the left chest and a larger graphic across the upper back. If your artwork has words beyond your name, decide on the French version early too — Montreal customers notice. How many to order

The mistake we see most is ordering for the shelf you imagine instead of the customers you have. A few rules:

  • One or two colours per piece for the first run. Every extra colour splits your sizes thinner.
  • Use a real size curve. Without other data, start from roughly 10% Small, 25% Medium, 35% Large, 20% XL and 10% 2XL, as explained in our size-run guide.
  • Order what you can sell in a season, then reorder what sold out. Your artwork stays on file, so a reorder is an email, not a new project.

Work back from your launch date

Merch usually has a date: an anniversary, a new location, a holiday market, a collab. Plan backwards from it:

  1. Artwork and quantities. Send your logo or graphics, the pieces and the rough quantities. We reply with a quote.
  2. Mockup approval. You approve a photoreal mockup of every piece before anything is printed. This is where you catch a colour or a size that looks wrong.
  3. Production. Standard turnaround is 5–10 business days from approved artwork. A 3-day rush is available for 25% extra.
  4. Pickup or delivery. Pick up in Sainte-Anne-de-Bellevue, get free Montreal delivery on 25+ units, or ship anywhere in Canada.

If your date is fixed, start the conversation about three weeks out. That leaves room for a round or two on the artwork and the full production window, without paying for rush. Tell us the date when you ask for a quote and we will confirm whether it works.

Merch ordering checklist

  1. Pick the pieces: start with two to four.
  2. Pick the blanks that feel like retail, and one or two colours each.
  3. Prepare the artwork: small front, larger back, simple cap mark, French version of any wording.
  4. Set quantities from a size curve and what you can sell in a season.
  5. Get a real quote and work out your cost per piece before setting prices.
  6. Approve the mockups.
  7. Confirm your date in writing.
  8. Reorder what sells — your files stay on file.

Questions we get from cafés and restaurants

What is the minimum order?

Five pieces, counted across the whole order. You can mix tees, crewnecks and caps to reach it.

Can staff shirts and retail merch go on one order?

Yes. Many businesses order a uniform tee for staff and a retail tee for the shelf at the same time, and we quote them together.

Can we see it before we commit?

Yes. You approve a photoreal mockup of every piece before we print anything.
