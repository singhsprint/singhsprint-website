Ordering custom team jerseys: names, numbers & league deadlines

Written for the team manager who volunteered to "handle the jerseys" — how names and numbers actually get on a jersey, the one spreadsheet that prevents 90% of order problems, and how far ahead of your season opener you really need to order.

9 min read Singhs Print · Published July 2026 Filed under: Teams, Jerseys

Jersey orders fail differently than t-shirt orders. A bulk tee order goes wrong by a few sizes; a jersey order goes wrong by a misspelled surname on the back of one specific player's jersey, discovered at the season opener. The difference is personalization: every unit in a roster order is unique, so every unit is a chance for an error. This guide covers the decisions and the paperwork that keep a roster order clean — based on the team orders we run for Montreal-area hockey, soccer, softball, and rec leagues.

The two ways names and numbers get on a jersey

Almost every question about jersey pricing and durability comes down to which of these two routes you're on.

Sublimation: the design is the jersey

With a sublimated jersey, the entire design — colours, stripes, logos, sponsor marks, and each player's name and number — is dyed directly into the polyester fabric in one pass. Because the personalization is part of the design file rather than a physical add-on, a roster of 18 unique name/number combinations costs the same to produce as 18 identical jerseys. Nothing sits on top of the fabric, so there's nothing to crack or peel in the wash. The trade-offs: it only works on polyester (which is what performance jerseys are made of anyway), and it's a cut-and-sew product, so lead times are longer.

Heat-press vinyl or twill on a stock jersey

The classic route: you pick a blank jersey from a catalog (poly mesh, pique, or a replica cut), and names and numbers are heat-applied as vinyl or stitched twill, priced per jersey as an add-on. It's faster and cheaper for small rosters, and it's the only option when a player joins mid-season and needs one jersey to match an existing set. The trade-off is durability: pressed vinyl sits on top of the fabric and can lift or crack over years of aggressive washing, and twill adds weight.

Rule of thumb: if every player gets a name and a unique number and you'll reorder the same design next season, sublimation usually wins on total cost — the per-jersey personalization fees on the heat-press route add up fast across a full roster. If you just need numbers on 12 shirts for a summer league, stock jerseys with pressed numbers are the sensible call.

The roster spreadsheet: one file, four columns

Every jersey problem we've ever had to fix traces back to roster data arriving in fragments — a text with three names, an email with two more, a size change in a voicemail. The fix is boring and it works: one spreadsheet, one owner (you), submitted once.

  • Player name, exactly as it should print. Capitalization included. "BEAULIEU-TREMBLAY" needs to fit; long surnames may need a condensed font — flag anything over 12 characters.
  • Number. Check for duplicates before you send it. Your printer will catch them, but that costs you a revision cycle.
  • Size. Collected from the player, not guessed. Jersey cuts run different from t-shirts — ask your printer for the size chart of the specific jersey model and circulate it. Our size-run guide covers what to do when a few players don't respond.
  • Goalie / special cut flag. Hockey and soccer goalkeepers often need a different cut or colour — and leagues typically require the keeper to contrast with the field players.

Then have one other person proofread the sheet against the registration list before it goes out. Every misspelled name we've ever printed was spelled that way in the file we received.

Numbering rules your league may enforce

Rules vary by sport and league, so treat this as a checklist of things to look up in your league handbook before you approve artwork — not as the rules themselves:

  • Unique numbers per roster — nearly universal, and some leagues restrict the allowed range (e.g. 0–99, or no "00").
  • Number placement and minimum size — many leagues specify a back number of a minimum height, and some require a smaller front or sleeve number.
  • Contrast — numbers must be clearly legible against the jersey colour; officials can refuse a kit they can't read.
  • Home / away colour rules — some leagues require a light and a dark set, which doubles your order and your budget line.
  • Sponsor logo restrictions — youth leagues in particular may limit sponsor placement or size.

The timeline, working back from your first game

Across the industry, custom sublimated uniforms typically take 2–4 weeks to produce after the design is approved, and the design/revision phase adds 1–2 weeks before that — which is why the standard advice is to start 6–8 weeks before you need jerseys in hand. Fall is the crunch: from September to January, demand across the industry stretches production times further. Here's the schedule we recommend:

Weeks before first gameWhat should be happening
8 weeksRequest quotes, pick jersey model and decoration route, check league rules

5–2 weeksProduction (sublimated cut-and-sew: 3–5 weeks; stock + press: 1–2 weeks)
1 weekDelivery buffer; check every jersey against the roster sheet on arrival
Ordering for a September league start? Approve artwork before mid-July. Every week you slip past that eats your buffer, and early-fall production queues are the longest of the year.

What a roster order costs

Treat these as illustrative ranges to frame your budget, not a quote — jersey model, quantity, and decoration complexity move the number, and industry-wide pricing for custom team jerseys spans roughly $15 to $80+ per jersey depending on those choices. For a typical adult rec-league roster of 15–20:

  • Stock jersey + pressed numbers only: often the budget floor — entry-level team sets commonly land in the range of $18–$28 per jersey at small quantities, less at volume.
  • Stock jersey + names and numbers: add a per-jersey personalization charge on top of the blank and numbering.
  • Fully sublimated custom kit: mid-range sublimated jerseys generally sit in the middle of that industry span, with names, numbers, and sponsor logos included in the price rather than added on.

The comparison that matters isn't jersey vs jersey — it's total roster cost over the seasons you'll use the design. A sublimated set that costs more up front but survives three seasons without a reprint frequently beats a cheaper set replaced every year. Five mistakes that wreck jersey orders

  1. No spares. Order 2–3 blank-backed or unnumbered spares in common sizes. A mid-season recruit or a destroyed jersey otherwise means a one-off order at the worst per-unit price.
  2. Approving the mockup without the roster on it. Approve a proof that shows actual names and numbers, not "PLAYER 00" placeholders.
  3. Collecting sizes by memory. Circulate the size chart; make players answer for themselves in writing.
  4. Ignoring the goalkeeper until the end. Different cut, different colour, sometimes different supplier lead time. Spec it in week one.
  5. Ordering exactly at the deadline. Shipping delays and one revision cycle are normal, not exceptional. The one-week buffer is not optional.
